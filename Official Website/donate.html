Where to donate

Novaris is free, has no accounts, and has nothing to sell you. Donations are the cleanest way to keep it that way: they create no obligation, they leave no record tied to your browsing, and they buy you no say in what gets built.

Note for the project owner. Both payment buttons below are deliberately inert until real details are supplied. Open js/config.js and fill in the PayPal and Cash App fields, then set each matching enabled flag to true. Until then they are visibly disabled rather than pointing at an account that does not exist.

What donations pay for

Being specific here matters, because a vague funding page is a way of avoiding scrutiny.

  • Code signing certificates. These are the most direct cost. A signed installer is what stops Windows SmartScreen from warning every person who downloads Novaris for the first time.
  • Release hosting and the update feed. Every update you download passes through this. It has to keep running, quietly, forever.
  • The domain and the certificate for it. Renewed annually, and not optional.
  • Testing on real Windows machines. A meaningful part of the work is finding out what a feature actually does in the packaged build rather than in a developer's copy.

Donations do not buy a support contract, a response time, a feature, or a say in the roadmap. If you want a feature built, ask on the issue tracker. If you want a bug fixed, that does not require money either.

What you get

A sincere thank you, and the knowledge that an independent browser with no accounts and no telemetry kept existing. There is no badge, no tier, no early access, and no list of donors published anywhere.

Why a project like this needs help

Building a browser is not hard because of the code. It is hard because of the long tail: Chromium ships security fixes continuously, someone has to track them and test that each one has not broken the layer built on top of it. That is years of unglamorous work, and it does not fit neatly into a sponsorship slide.

If you would rather not donate, the most useful thing costs nothing: install it, use it for a week, and report what broke. Real usage finds real bugs, and we would far rather have those than money.
