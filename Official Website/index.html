Everything a browser should already be doing

Each of these was built and then tested against the packaged build, not against a mock-up. Where something could not be done honestly, it is listed under Limits instead of being quietly left out.

Vertical tabs and workspaces

Tabs down the left, grouped, and organised into workspaces that hold those groups. Drag to reorder, search every open tab at once, and hover for a preview before you switch.

Ads blocked at the request

Blocking happens before the request leaves your PC, not by hiding things afterwards. Three levels, custom filters, and a live count of what was stopped and who was behind it.

Scam sites stopped before they load

Hosts reported as fraud or malware are blocked with the reason shown. A listed host is blocked exactly; the platform hosting it is never blocked by association.

A vault that stays on your PC

Each login is sealed individually with AES-256-GCM under an Argon2id key, bound to the site it belongs to. A fill is refused on any other host, so a fake sign-in page cannot collect it.

Website Trust Card

One card per site: is the connection encrypted, what can it do, which extensions can read the page, and does the address imitate a brand you trust. Checked entirely on your device.

Gaming Mode

Unloads every tab you are not looking at instead of merely hiding it, refuses notifications outright, and stops the spellchecker. The cost is stated in the interface, not discovered later.

Private windows

A separate window on storage that is never written to disk, with its own randomly named partition so two private windows cannot see each other. Nothing survives closing it.

Tab transfer, device to device

Send a tab to another device on your network with no server involved. Encrypted on the sending PC before it moves, authorised by a pairing code, and no copy kept afterwards.

Media controls and picture in picture

A control bar for whatever the page is playing, with picture-in-picture that works from any site. The page stays in charge of its own state, so a playlist advancing by itself is followed.

A side panel for your apps

Discord, YouTube, Spotify, Canvas and Mail stay open beside your tabs instead of taking one. Only the app you are looking at is loaded, so the rail costs a tab rather than eleven.

Saved pages for offline reading

Keep an article to read on a plane. The capture is placed in a sandbox with no script execution and no same-origin access, and it carries the address and time it was taken.

Updates you can check yourself

Every release is published with its size and a SHA-512 hash, and the app verifies the download before installing it. No forced updates, no background phone-home.

What we measured, rather than what we claim

Most privacy pages list wins. These numbers come from running the packaged build and watching what it actually did.

0 Google endpoints

A Chromium network log recorded over a session visiting three sites produced 39 requests to exactly 3 hosts — all page content. No Safe Browsing pings, no component updater, no variations, no autofill upload, no telemetry.

152 The engine is Chrome's

Novaris renders every page with Chromium 152 through Electron 44.4.5. It is the same engine, with the Google services switched off around it.

✓ Cookies already partitioned

A cookie set on one origin was not sent when a second origin requested it. That matches Chrome's default, and it is why the Trust Card can show you a site is not quietly reading another site's session.

▣ The vault has no escape hatch

Once a browser password is set, it is a hard lock. There is no "continue without unlocking". The only way past it destroys the vault rather than opening it.

Tab transfer, device to device

  • Send a tab to another device on your network with no server, no account, and nothing hosted anywhere.
  • Encrypted on the sending PC with AES-256-GCM before it moves, so nothing readable crosses the network.
  • A 100-bit pairing code authorises the transfer, compared in constant time, and stops working after ten minutes.
  • Only local network addresses can be reached, so Novaris cannot be used to make an outward connection.

Side panel apps, media controls and saved pages

  • Discord, YouTube, Spotify, Canvas, Mail and a Notes scratchpad stay open beside your tabs.
  • Media controls for whatever the page is playing, with working picture-in-picture from any site.
  • Saved pages for offline reading, sandboxed so a capture cannot reach your credentials.
  • Picture-in-picture is driven by Novaris rather than the page, because a browser requires a real click.

Vertical tabs and workspaces

  • Tabs down the left, grouped, organised into workspaces that hold those groups.
  • Drag to reorder or move between groups, search every open tab at once, hover for a preview.
  • Right-click to duplicate, mute, close, or move a tab to another group or workspace.
  • Deleting a group keeps its tabs. Deleting a workspace keeps its groups. Neither destroys work.

Privacy and safety

  • Local phishing detection that checks whether an address is imitating a brand you trust, with the reasons shown.
  • The Website Trust Card: encryption, permissions, extensions with access, and advertising stopped, per site.
  • Private windows on storage that is never written to disk, with their own random partition each.
  • Gaming Mode unloads inactive tabs, refuses notifications, and stops the spellchecker.

What it cannot do

A privacy browser that only lists its wins is telling you half the story. These are the limits we have measured and are not pretending to have solved.

Known limits

Your public IP address can be disclosed by a website A site that configures a STUN server can obtain your public address through WebRTC, with no permission prompt. Chromium's own IP handling switches were tested three different ways and do not prevent it. The real mitigations are routing your traffic through an anonymising network, or an extension that removes WebRTC per site. Novaris does not claim to stop this.
Chrome Web Store extensions do not install directly Google exposes no public install API to third parties, so "Add to Chrome" cannot be made to work honestly. Novaris detects Manifest V3 extensions, labels the ones whose background logic will not run, and tells you why instead of letting them install and quietly do nothing. Manifest V2 extensions work.
Passkeys can be created but not yet used A website can register a passkey into Windows Hello through Novaris today, and the platform authenticator is available. Signing in with one needs an account chooser that Electron does not draw and offers no way for a browser to substitute. That is an upstream gap, not a setting.
Phishing detection reads the address, not the reputation It catches hostname impersonation, which is the large class of attacks. It is not a reputation service, so a brand-new malicious domain with an unusual name can still get through. Nothing you visit is sent anywhere to produce a verdict.
Tab transfer needs both devices on one network Sending a tab to a device elsewhere while you are away from home is not something Novaris can do, because that needs a server you would have to run.

Common questions

Is Novaris really Chrome, or a rebrand of it?

It uses the same rendering engine, Chromium, which is what Chrome is built on. What Novaris changes is everything around it: which network requests are allowed, what is stored and where, and what a website is told about you. Saying "it is Chromium" is the honest description; claiming to be Chrome would not be.

Does it phone home to Google?

No. We measured it rather than trusting the configuration: a network log over a real session showed no Google endpoint being contacted at all. The Trust Card in Settings reports what was actually observed, and notes that it is produced on your device.

What happens to my passwords?

You choose whether to move them. Novaris never reads a Chrome or Edge password database on its own; importing is a deliberate step, and you review what comes across. Once saved, each login is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from your browser password using Argon2id, and is only ever filled back into the site it was saved for.

Is there an account or a subscription?

No account, and nothing to pay for. There is no server component, which is also why there is nothing to log you in to.

Will it force an update on me?

It asks first, shows the download size, and only installs when you agree. Every release publishes a SHA-512 hash and the app verifies the download before installing it. Uninstalling removes your profile, so the next install is a clean first run.

Can I use my Chrome or Edge extensions?

Manifest V2 extensions work. Most current extensions are Manifest V3, whose background logic runs in a service worker that Electron does not execute. Novaris detects that from the manifest, warns you before you install, and labels it afterwards, so you are never left with an extension that appears installed and does nothing.
