Why this website shows advertising

Last updated: 27 September 2026

Short answer. The application has no accounts, no telemetry and no subscription, so it cannot be funded by selling your data. This website carries advertising and accepts donations instead. Ads appear only after you allow them, and the application itself contains no advertising of any kind.

The application and this website are different

It is worth being precise, because a privacy product that carries advertising on its own website gives people an easy and fair reason to distrust everything else it says.

Inside the application, there is no advertising, no analytics and no network call to any third party. We measured this rather than assuming it: a Chromium network log over a session visiting three unrelated sites recorded 39 requests to exactly three hosts, every one of them page content. No telemetry, no Safe Browsing pings, no component updater, no autofill upload. The Trust Card inside the app tells you the same thing while you browse, and it works with no internet connection because it is computed on your machine.

On this website, you are reading HTML served from a web host, and the only way to keep it running is to pay for the host, the domain and the release infrastructure. That is what the advertising pays for.

Why not the other options

Not a subscription

A subscription would mean accounts, which means a server, which means a database of people and a permanent obligation to protect it. The single most valuable property this project has is that there is nothing to leak. A paywall would trade it away for money, and it would also put the browser behind a login for the people least able to pay.

Not selling data

There is no data to sell. Not because of a policy we might change, but because the application does not collect any.

Not enterprise sponsorship, yet

Corporate sponsorship is a reasonable way to fund an independent browser, and it would carry fewer privacy costs than advertising. It is not in place yet, and committing to it later is easier than pretending to it now.

What allowing advertising actually does

If you press Allow advertising, the Google AdSense script is added to the page. That script and its partners may set cookies, including Google's advertising identifiers, and may use them to measure whether an advert was shown and to limit how often you see the same one. Where you have not opted out of personalisation, they may also use your activity on this and other sites to select advertising.

The full list is in the cookie policy.

What advertising does not do

  • It does not appear anywhere inside the application.
  • It does not get access to your browsing, bookmarks, passwords or history.
  • It does not run unless you allow it, and the script is removed if you decline afterwards.
  • It is not used to decide which version of Novaris you are offered. There is only one.

The awkward part, stated plainly

There is a real inconsistency here and it is not worth pretending otherwise. This page argues that tracking is a problem, and this page's advertising partner sets an identifier for you. Both things are true at once.

The distinction we can defend is narrow but real: the advertising is on the marketing site, where you have a choice and a visible control, and it never reaches the software you install. A browser that tracked you would be a different and much worse thing, and we have built the measurements to show we have not done that.

If you would rather this site did not carry advertising at all, declining is a single click and the site is fully readable without it. We would rather lose the impression than collect it without asking.

Donating instead

A donation keeps the site independent of advertising entirely, and there is nothing behind it either. It goes to server costs and code signing certificates, and it carries no implication that you have bought a say in what the browser does.
